# New Samadore

## Premise

Nestled in the lush landscapes of New Guinea, the nation of New Samadore stands as a deliberate, ongoing experiment in coexistence between arriving gnomes and native human communities. Their peaceful integration is anchored by the **Harmony Pledge**, a foundational architecture originally designed to temper the brilliant, volatile extremes of gnomish nature.

When the Magic Gates opened across Earth in 1814, one tore open on the coast of eastern New Guinea, and the first gnomish refugees, pilgrims, and exiles from Ankaris stepped through onto an unfamiliar shore already home to the Melanesian peoples of New Guinea and its surrounding waters. The gnomes had fled a collapsing Ankarene polity, generations of factional excess having torn it apart, and they arrived carrying little more than the conviction that whatever they built next would not repeat that failure.

The gate has never closed. Gnomish arrival did not end with that first crossing but continues to this day, a steady rather than sudden migration that Samador Anchorage now manages as deliberately as it manages everything else — throttled, encouraged, or redirected according to how much strain the coast can absorb without tipping its own Harmony spheres. New Samadore's gnomish population is still, in a real sense, being founded.

Settlement spread from the coastal gate along navigable shores and river mouths. Within a generation, gnomes and their Melanesian neighbors had built something neither people had built alone: a state whose laws, economy, and culture are explicitly engineered to keep a fundamentally unstable founding population from tearing itself apart.

## The Gnomes of New Samadore

Gnomes stand roughly three to four feet tall, long-lived by human standards, and prone to a kind of collective, contagious obsession that Ankarene gnomish culture never fully solved for. A gnomish institution does not drift gradually out of balance; it seizes on something — a ledger system, a vein of ore, a school of economic theory, a style of masonry — and pursues it with total, unembarrassed commitment until the rest of the country is straining to compensate.

New Samadore's own short history is already dotted with these episodes: a management reform so thorough it choked off innovation even as it eliminated waste; an extraction boom that gutted the plantations it grew alongside; a wave of civic construction that pulled labor out of the fields until granaries ran thin; a financial vogue for speculative instruments that flooded state coffers while draining industry of hard reserves. None of these were acts of malice or even carelessness — each was, in the moment, gnomish brilliance running exactly as designed, just without a brake. Left unchecked, the same instinct that builds a Woven Monument in one season can, in the next, produce something as bewildering as a political faction that campaigns in the same breath for total anarchism and a permanent militarized state — sincerely, and to considerable gnomish applause.

Gnomish curiosity carries the same intensity into personal conduct. A gnome's fascination with a neighbor, a stranger, or a sacred rite can escalate from charming to invasive to genuinely offensive within a single conversation, entirely without ill intent — a trait that has caused real diplomatic damage among New Samadore's Melanesian citizens and, further inland, among highlanders who have had far less time to get used to it.

## The Harmony Pledge

The Harmony Pledge is New Samadore's state religion — though "religion" undersells what is, in practice, a constitutional operating system. It has no single deity and promises no afterlife. Its object of devotion is balance itself, and it is administered less like a faith and more like a set of instruments a country's leadership is expected to read every day.

### The Three Spheres

The Pledge tracks the health of the nation across three spheres, each scored on a continuous scale from total collapse into rigid order at one end to total collapse into chaos at the other:

| Sphere                  | Order Extreme                                     | Chaos Extreme                              |
|-------------------------|---------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| **Societal Harmony**    | Stratified — caste hardens into law               | Antinomian — no norm or authority holds    |
| **Economic Harmony**    | Cartelised — a handful of concerns own everything | Febrile — speculative, directionless churn |
| **Legislative Harmony** | Despotic — power concentrates absolutely          | Anaemic — the state cannot act at all      |

A sphere resting near the center is declared **Harmonious**. As it drifts toward either pole it is declared **Tilting**, a visible warning that intervention is needed. Should any sphere ever reach its full extreme — a flat 0 or 100 — the Pledge itself is considered to have failed, with consequences the Wardenry describes, without exaggeration, as catastrophic.

What pushes a sphere toward one pole or the other is everything that happens in the country: which laws are on the books, how the population is composed, what the economy is doing, and — not least — which of the gnomish fixations described above happens to be sweeping the nation that decade. A construction mania drags Societal Harmony one way; a cartel of trading houses drags Economic Harmony another. Part of what the Pledge measures is simply New Samadore governing itself in real time.

### The Wardenry

The Pledge is maintained by the **Wardenry**, an order of Warden-magistrates who function as clergy, civil administrators, and something close to a national early-warning system all at once. Wardens read the three spheres the way a physician reads vital signs, and they hold real institutional power: they can call for a sector's fixation to be reined in, dampened, or — controversially — embraced and ridden out, and they administer the confession halls where citizens (gnomish and human alike) publicly declare intentions before major undertakings, a practice meant to catch a coming fixation before it becomes a crisis rather than after.

The Pledge counts adherents the way any religion does, and conversion is not automatic. Melanesian communities along the coast have taken up the Pledge in large numbers, but trust is not unconditional — gnomish scholars who treated a solemn ancestral rite as "an interactive puzzle," interrupting prayers with rapid-fire questions, did lasting damage to conversion in the community involved. Elsewhere, integration has produced something stranger and more durable than simple conversion: Melanesian laborers who have folded gnomish industrial machinery directly into animist practice, painting sacred geometries onto steam engines and refusing to run them on "inauspicious" days, to gnomish engineers' considerable frustration. The Wardenry has generally chosen to tolerate this syncretism rather than fight it, on the theory that a hybrid faith practiced sincerely does more for Societal Harmony than a purer one practiced resentfully.

### Abandonment and Reclamation

The Pledge is not universally beloved. A recurring faction within the Assembly — usually gnomish, usually young — argues that the Pledge is a cage on true gnomish potential and pushes for its formal abandonment through the enactment of state atheism. Every serious attempt to do this has been treated by the country's institutions as an emergency: abandoning the Pledge triggers a decade-long **Abandoned Pledge** condition that leaves the nation measurably worse off across the board, a cost severe enough that, historically, exhausted appetite for unchecked extremity has always eventually produced a popular movement to reclaim the Pledge rather than live without it.

## Government and Institutions

New Samadore is governed through a conventional apparatus of law — its distribution of power, bureaucracy, internal security, courts, and economic policy are all set through ordinary legislative processes — but every one of those institutions operates, in effect, as an instrument of the Harmony Pledge. Church and state are formally entangled: the Wardenry sits alongside, not beneath, the civil administration, and a law that shifts how power is distributed or how the bureaucracy runs registers immediately in the Pledge's own readings.

One legal peculiarity stands out to outsiders: New Samadore maintains a dedicated body of law specifically regulating magic users, gnomish and human alike, treating spellcasting as a licensed and supervised civic function rather than a private or purely religious matter — one more expression of a country that would rather regulate a volatile force in advance than clean up after it.

## Integration of the Melanesian Coast

The Melanesian communities nearest the original gate site were the first to be drawn into New Samadore, and after two decades they are, for practical purposes, simply New Samadorian: citizens under the same law, represented in the same Assembly, worshipping the Pledge in numbers alongside gnomes, their institutions and grievances woven into the ordinary business of governance rather than treated as a separate "integration project." The friction that remains — gnomish overfamiliarity, occasional religious offense, factories run on spirit-appeasing schedules — is managed the way any other source of disharmony is managed: through the Wardenry, not through separate policy.

## The Veiled Interior

The highlands are a different matter entirely, and as of yet unresolved. Contact proceeds in stages: an **expedition** first ventures inland, often at real cost — crocodile attacks at river crossings, disorientation in trackless jungle, disease, and at least one expedition that simply never returned. Once a route holds, a **scouting** phase follows to properly survey what expeditions found, which is how New Samadore first confirmed the scale of what lies inland: not a scattered population but a "lived-in world of its own," organized and numerous enough that current estimates put the highland Melanesian population near a million — comparable to, and by some counts exceeding, New Samadore's own settled population.

Where contact holds, formal **negotiations** begin, moving in turn through discussions, alignment, and implementation before a highland community formally joins the nation. None of it is simple. Negotiating envoys have vanished under circumstances the capital only ever hears about as rumor, feeding gnomish anxieties about "extreme cultural practices" in the interior that may be exaggerated, misunderstood, or worse — nobody in Samador Anchorage claims to know which. Highland chiefs have flatly rejected the stationing of New Samadorian troops on their land as occupation dressed up as partnership. The Assembly itself nearly derailed one round of talks after leaked drafts revealed legislative loopholes crafted to tolerate highland rites the Wardenry judged too sacred to legislate away and hardliners judged too gruesome to legalize. Disease outbreaks have triggered highland border closures and coastal quarantines in the same breath, each side suspicious the other engineered the crisis. Even cartography is a battlefield: New Samadore's surveyors draw fixed coordinate lines through territory highland chiefs have always defined by blood ties and seasonal landmarks, and more than one promising negotiation has stalled over a resource vein or mountain pass claimed by both a chief's ancestry and a surveyor's map.

Domestic politics has not stayed out of it. Orators and playwrights have built careers arguing for integration; other voices in New Samadore's own art establishment have denounced "interior" cultural influence in openly xenophobic terms, insisting it has no place in a properly New Samadorian canon — a prejudice the Wardenry has been notably slow to correct, since Societal Harmony scores, inconveniently, do not distinguish a righteous grievance from a bigoted one.

Every highland community that has completed the process is counted a full success: a "shared future" chosen, in the Wardenry's language, rather than imposed. Whether New Samadore can extend that choice to a million more people without its own harmony spheres buckling under the strain is, at the time of writing, the open question the entire nation is still answering.

## Foreign Relations

New Samadore is young and preoccupied — its most consequential foreign relations are not with distant powers but with the highland communities inside its own claimed borders. The Wardenry approaches both the interior and the wider, newly reachable world with the same instinct: caution against the kind of unchecked zealotry, colonial or otherwise, the Pledge exists to prevent. Whether that caution survives contact with larger and hungrier powers, once they take notice of a small nation sitting on a strategically placed gate, remains untested.
