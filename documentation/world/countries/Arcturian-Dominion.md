# Arcturian Dominion

## Premise

Emerging through a Gate that tore open near the Zambian interior, the elves of the Arcturian Dominion did not arrive as refugees looking for a place to survive. They arrived as an expedition sent by the Arcturian Empire, their home polity on Ankaris, with a doctrine already fully formed, and within a generation they had turned that doctrine into a state. Where the gnomes of New Samadore stepped onto an unfamiliar shore with nothing but the conviction that their old failures would not repeat themselves, the elves of the Dominion stepped onto the Zambian plateau already certain of exactly what came next: the old order of Ankaris, purified and reasserted, this time on a continent that had never heard of it. The Dominion is, by its own law and the Empire's, a client state rather than a sovereign one — Earth's foothold of a much older and much larger power that has never set foot through the Gate itself, and has so far never needed to.

They found the interior already organized under the hierarchical statecraft of the Lunda Kingdoms — a network of tributary chieftaincies bound by ritual, kinship, and the memory-keeping of the Mbudye, the royal historians who legitimized every Lunda ruler's claim to authority. The Dominion did not dismantle this system. It climbed inside it. Elven emissaries, missionaries, and — where persuasion stalled — soldiers spent a generation converting and then annexing the Lunda states one by one, leaving the old titles and councils standing wherever they could be made useful and replacing only what sat at the very top. Further south, the Dominion moved differently: rather than convert and absorb the Tswana peoples outright, it folded their polities into a protectorate, a looser arrangement of treaties, garrisons, and indirect rule that extracts loyalty without demanding total religious conversion. Both approaches have brought the Dominion into contact — and increasingly into conflict — with the Portuguese colonies to the west and the British colonies to the south, whose own colonial ambitions in the region did not anticipate a theocratic elven empire arriving from another world to compete for the same ground. The border skirmishes that have followed have cost the Dominion little that it will admit to, and have done more than any sermon to cement its reputation among its neighbors: cunning, patient, and very hard to dislodge once it has taken root.

## The Elves of the Arcturian Dominion

Dominion elves are long-lived even by the standard of their kind on Ankaris, tall and unhurried, and carry themselves with the settled confidence of a people who have never seriously entertained the possibility that they might be wrong about their place in the world. They do not share the gnomes' fixational instability — a Dominion institution does not seize on a fashion and run it into the ground within a season — but the trade-off is a different kind of rigidity: elven culture changes its mind slowly, deliberately, and usually only after a crisis has made the old position untenable. What looks like patience to an elf reads, to almost everyone the Dominion rules, as an unwillingness to be argued with.

Central to elven self-understanding is a phenomenon the Dominion calls the Gilding: a fine, golden luminescence that rises in an elf's eyes and along the skin when they draw on mana, visible to anyone standing close enough and unmistakable to anyone who has been taught what it means. No other race on Earth or Ankaris reliably produces it. To an outsider it is simply a striking side effect of elven spellcraft. To the Dominion it is proof — not metaphor, not custom, but observable, repeatable proof — that magic itself has chosen the elven people, and the Golden Blessing exists to give that proof a theology.

## The Golden Blessing

The Golden Blessing is the Dominion's state religion, and unlike New Samadore's Harmony Pledge it is not a set of instruments for managing instability — it is a claim about the fixed order of the world, and the Dominion's entire civic life is built to keep restating that claim. It has no single named god; devotion is directed instead at the Aurum, the radiance itself, understood not as a personality to petition but as a current running through creation that happens to surface, visibly, only in elven blood. Where the Wardenry reads three spheres every day looking for drift, the Auric Clergy reads the world for confirmation, and generally finds it, because the doctrine is built to only ever be confirmed.

The Clergy is organized beneath the Lumenarch, the Dominion's head of state and highest priest at once — church and throne are not merely entangled here, as in New Samadore, but identical. Beneath the Lumenarch, Auric priests serve simultaneously as missionaries, magistrates, and censors of magical practice, since regulating who is permitted to cast spells is, in Dominion law, indistinguishable from regulating who is permitted to worship correctly. Conversion of the conquered is not treated as separate from governance; it is governance, carried out through the same missionary infrastructure that produced the Golden Conversion of the Lunda states — years of preaching, political pressure, and outright violence that ended, eventually, with Lunda elders kneeling beside elven priests under banners the Dominion calls unifying and the Mbudye, more quietly, call the price of survival.

It is an article of Auric doctrine that the Gilding cannot appear in non-elven blood. It is also, quietly, an article of Auric practice that on the rare recorded occasions when it has, the matter is not investigated publicly. Clergy who have pressed the question have found their careers ending abruptly and without explanation. Nobody outside the Clergy's upper ranks knows whether this is because the doctrine is being protected from an inconvenient truth, or because it never had to be.

## Elven Supremacy

Dominion society runs on Elven Supremacy, a legal and social order that grants elves an unassailable monopoly on power, prestige, and magic, and confines every other people within its borders to lower echelons explicitly barred from the clergy, the aristocracy, and the practice of spellcraft. The system is presented, and largely believed by the elves who benefit from it, as the natural consequence of the Gilding rather than as a choice — if magic itself marks who is fit to rule, then a state that puts elves in charge of magic, law, and worship alike is simply describing reality rather than imposing it.

In practice this "natural" order requires constant enforcement. Human, Lunda, Tswana, and other subjects who show unusual aptitude, ambition, or simple competence are steered — sometimes gently, sometimes not — away from any path that would test the doctrine's edges. Magical talent in a non-elf is the single most dangerous thing a Dominion subject can be caught possessing, not because the Clergy is certain it does not exist, but because the entire architecture of the state depends on nobody being able to prove that it does.

## Government and Institutions

The Dominion is, formally, a theocracy, and every organ of state exists to serve and be legitimized by the Golden Blessing. Where the Dominion rules directly, elven governors answer to the Auric Clergy rather than to any separate civil bureaucracy. Where it rules through others — as it does across most of the Lunda Kingdoms and the whole of the Tswana protectorate — it prefers co-option to demolition, keeping local titles, councils, and chieftaincies intact and legally subordinate rather than replacing them wholesale, because a functioning local hierarchy conquered is cheaper to maintain than one destroyed and rebuilt from nothing. The result is a state that looks, on a map, like a single unified Dominion, and functions, in practice, as a core of direct elven rule surrounded by a widening ring of client kingdoms, converted states, and treaty protectorates, each bound to the center by a different mixture of faith, force, and convenience.

## The Arcturian Empire

For all the authority the Lumenarch wields over Lunda kings and Tswana chiefs, the Dominion answers, in turn, to someone else. It holds the formal standing of a dominion of the Arcturian Empire — bound to the Empire's foreign policy, its markets, and ultimately its will, in exchange for the legitimacy, doctrine, and (when the Empire judges it worth the cost) the material support of the oldest and largest Arcturian polity on Ankaris. The Empire itself has never sent an army through the Zambian Gate; it has never needed to. A Lumenarch who governs well, converts steadily, and keeps the Gilding's secrets is left almost entirely alone. What the relationship actually costs the Dominion in practice — tribute, doctrine, deference in matters the Empire considers its own — and what leverage, if any, the Dominion holds in return, is a live and unsettled question rather than a fixed arrangement, and one the Lumenarch's court has never had to test against real imperial displeasure.

That the Dominion has never been tested is not the same as the relationship being comfortable. Every Auric priest is trained to describe the Empire as the source the Golden Blessing flows from rather than merely a distant overlord, but the distinction matters less to a Lumenarch weighing whether an unpopular imperial directive is worth enforcing than it does to the priests saying it. Strategies for managing the relationship with the Empire — how much autonomy to cultivate, what the Dominion owes versus what it can withhold, and whether outright independence from Ankaris is a live goal, a distant fantasy, or a heresy no Lumenarch could survive proposing — are deliberately left undeveloped here and are intended as a later addition to this document, once the mechanics for that relationship exist.

## The Lunda Kingdoms and the Golden Conversion

The Lunda Kingdoms — the core Lunda state alongside the related Chokwe, Kazembe, and Kasanje polities — were the Dominion's first and hardest-won territory, and remain its most closely managed. Conquest alone was never the goal; conversion to the Golden Blessing was, since only a converted population can be integrated under Dominion law rather than merely occupied by Dominion soldiers. The Auric Clergy conducts this conversion through the Mbudye, the Lunda's own venerated council of interpreters and historians, weaving Auric scripture into Lunda tradition rather than replacing it outright — a strategy that has done real damage to the Mbudye's independence even as it has kept outright revolt from becoming permanent.

Dominion missionaries in the field choose, state by state, how hard to push:

| Approach | Yearly Support | Yearly Conversion |
|----------|----------------|--------------------|
| **Aggressive** | −1% | +3% |
| **Balanced** | no change | +2% |
| **Passive** | +1% | +1% |

Pushed too hard, too fast, the Lunda states have shown they can set aside their own rivalries and rise together — the Dominion calls this the Drums of Unity, and treats a unified Lunda uprising as the single costliest failure mode its Zambian territory can produce. Even short of open revolt, the Dominion faces the Bambudye, a secret resistance society operating in the shadow of the official Mbudye, whose operatives have assassinated Dominion officials, generals, admirals, and politicians alike, defaced Golden Blessing temples, and sabotaged supply lines during moments of open unrest. The Dominion's response to any of this — public execution, quiet investigation, censorship, or reprisal against an entire village — is itself a fresh site of friction, since every option the Lumenarch's officials reach for tends to either radicalize the next generation of Bambudye recruits or convince Dominion hardliners that the Clergy is being too gentle by half.

A Lunda state that completes conversion is not yet a Dominion state. Integration follows as its own extended process — roughly a year of binding scrolls, administrative absorption, and ceremony — after which the kingdom's laws and territory formally become the Dominion's own, in faith and in fact, even as its people remain confined to the lower tiers Elven Supremacy reserves for them.

## The Tswana Protectorate

South of the Lunda Kingdoms, the Dominion has taken a different approach entirely with the Tswana peoples, blending diplomacy and subjugation into a protectorate rather than a program of religious conversion. Tswana chiefs retain their titles, their courts, and a good deal of day-to-day authority over their own communities; what they have surrendered is control of foreign policy, military mobilization, and — increasingly — the mineral and mana-rich land beneath them, ceded through treaties negotiated from a position the Dominion made sure was never equal. It is a cheaper arrangement than the Lunda conquest, and a more fragile one: without the Golden Blessing's missionary infrastructure binding Tswana communities to the Dominion the way it binds the Lunda, protectorate loyalty runs only as deep as the last treaty's terms, and the British colonial administrations to the south — who have protectorate ambitions of their own in Tswana country — have not stopped testing exactly how deep that is.

## Foreign Relations

The Dominion's borders touch two established colonial powers, and it has treated both the same way it treats a newly conquered Lunda state: not as an equal to negotiate with, but as an obstacle to be probed, absorbed where possible, and outlasted where not. Clashes along the western frontier with Portuguese Angola and along the southern frontier with British territory have so far stayed limited — skirmishes and border incidents rather than declared wars — but each has left the Dominion more confident rather than less, since neither colonial power has yet managed to translate a tactical check into a lasting one. Whether that confidence survives a Portuguese or British government deciding the Dominion is worth a real campaign, rather than a border nuisance, is untested. What is already clear to every chancellery in the region is that the Dominion negotiates the way it converts: patiently, on its own terms, and rarely from a position it considers it might lose.

## Notes / open threads for future development

- **The Arcturian Empire relationship is unbuilt and is the priority open thread.** This document deliberately stops short of defining what the Dominion owes the Empire, what the Empire could plausibly demand or withhold, and what mechanisms (if any) would let the Dominion accumulate autonomy over time. Whether independence from Ankaris is ever a real option for the Dominion — versus a thought no Lumenarch could survive voicing — is likewise left open, to be answered when that content is built rather than assumed here.
- The suppressed question of the Gilding appearing outside elven blood is the doctrine's single load-bearing secret; a scandal that forced it into the open would strike at Elven Supremacy's entire justification, not just its enforcement.
- The Bambudye's relationship to the official Mbudye — infiltrators, disillusioned former members, or something the Mbudye quietly tolerates as insurance — is left open and could be developed either way.
- Dominion and New Samadore represent, without either state necessarily being aware of it, two opposite Ankarene answers to the same failure: one an operating system built to prevent any single order from ever becoming absolute, the other a doctrine built to prove one already has. A future point of contact between them — diplomatic, religious, or otherwise — would not need much invention to turn contentious.
- The Tswana Protectorate's looser, treaty-based loyalty versus the Lunda Kingdoms' deeper but harder-won religious integration gives the Dominion two different failure modes to draw on for future unrest — a protectorate defection playing very differently than a Lunda uprising.
